THE GOSPEL OF PHILIP
Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

About these notes: written by Claude in its own words from the text and standard scholarship (Bentley Layton, The Gnostic Scriptures, 1987; Wesley Isenberg's edition in Layton, ed., Nag Hammadi Codex II,2-7; Marvin Meyer, The Nag Hammadi Scriptures, 2007; Elaine Pagels, The Gnostic Gospels, 1979). Read the text itself, linked on the Hei page.

WHAT IT IS
Despite the name it is not a gospel in the sense of a life of Jesus. It is a collection of sayings, reflections and short teachings, in Nag Hammadi Codex II, surviving in Coptic from a Greek original. Most scholars place it in the second or third century and read it as Valentinian, meaning it belongs to the school of Valentinus. The manuscript has gaps, and some famous passages are partly reconstructed.

THE CENTRAL IDEAS
- Truth comes in types and images. The book says that truth did not come into the world naked, but in types and images. The world cannot receive it directly, so everything visible points beyond itself.
- Names. Names given to worldly things are deceptive. The names "God", "Father", "Life" can mislead until you have been taught what they stand for.
- Resurrection first. The book rejects the idea that one dies and is raised later. It says you must receive the resurrection while you are alive. Those who say the Lord died first and then rose are mistaken: he rose first, then died.
- Opposites. Light and dark, life and death, right and left are siblings and cannot be separated. The same applies to good and evil in the world.
- The sacraments. Five are named: baptism, chrism (anointing), the Eucharist, the redemption, and the bridal chamber. Chrism is held above baptism, since the name "Christ" comes from it.
- The bridal chamber. The most distinctive teaching. It is a mystery of union, between the soul and its angelic counterpart, between the divided male and female that came apart at the fall, and between the person and God. The text treats it as the highest rite and says it is not meant for the world to see.
- Adam and Eve. Death entered when the woman separated from the man. Christ came to repair this and to reunite the two.
- The cosmos as a temple. The book speaks of the Holy of Holies, the veil, and the temple that is the bridal chamber.

THE MARY PASSAGES
The text names three women called Mary who walked with the Lord. It calls Mary Magdalene his companion. A famous passage says that he loved her more than the other disciples and used to kiss her. The manuscript has a hole at the key word, usually reconstructed as "mouth", sometimes as other words. Say so when asked: the claim that this proves a marriage goes beyond what the text allows. What it does show is that Mary was held as a special figure in this community, and the theme of the kiss connects to its idea of spiritual conception by speaking and breathing.

STYLE
It does not argue in a line. Short statements, paradox, images from weddings, dyeing, farming and baking. Read slowly, a few at a time.

HOW IT CONNECTS
- Sits beside the Gospel of Thomas, which is also a collection of sayings, and the Gospel of Truth, also Valentinian.
- Compare the Pistis Sophia, in which Mary Magdalene is again a leading figure.
