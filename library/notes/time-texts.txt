THE TIME TEXTS: A SHORT READING LIST FOR ORTHOGONAL TIME
Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

About these notes: written by Claude in its own words from general knowledge of the works, not from copies. The authors are all long dead, but public-domain status depends on edition and translation, so I have not hosted anything. Verify each edition before uploading it. Each of these is an old attempt to say that time is not what the clock suggests.

J. M. E. McTaggart, "The Unreality of Time" (1908)
A famous argument that time cannot be real, because the idea of past, present and future contradicts itself. He distinguishes two ways of ordering events, one by "before and after" and one by "past, present and future," and argues the second cannot be coherent. Philosophers still argue over it. The closest thing here to the whole record on a turntable.

J. W. Dunne, An Experiment with Time (1927)
An aeronautical engineer reports that he had dreams of events that had not yet happened, and proposes a theory of "serial time," in which each moment of time is observed from a further dimension of time, without end. Dunne influenced J. B. Priestley and a generation of writers of time-themed fiction.

Henri Bergson, Time and Free Will (1889; English translation 1910)
Argues that time as felt, which Bergson calls duration, differs from the clock's divided time. Bergson appears in Dick's glossary.

Boethius, The Consolation of Philosophy (c. 524)
Defines eternity as the complete, simultaneous possession of unending life, and uses it to reconcile God's foreknowledge with human freedom. The classic account of a mind outside time.

P. D. Ouspensky, Tertium Organum (1912; English translation 1920)
A book about higher dimensions and the "fourth dimension" as time seen differently. Ouspensky later became Gurdjieff's chief interpreter, and both are on the Hei list.

C. H. Hinton, A New Era of Thought (1888) and The Fourth Dimension (1904)
Popularized the idea of a fourth spatial dimension, inspired a generation of occult and scientific thinkers, and introduced the word "tesseract."

HOW THEY CONNECT
- Dick's glossary defines orthogonal time as time at right angles to linear time, with all that has happened present at once. These texts are the long background to that idea.
- Wilson's non-local circuit uses quantum physics to point toward a similar claim.
- The Church of the Whole Record uses the image of a record on a turntable to say the same thing in a form anyone can picture.
