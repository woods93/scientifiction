PROMETHEUS RISING
Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

About these notes: written by Claude in its own words. They are based on the owner's copy of the New Falcon Publications edition (Robert Anton Wilson, introduced by Israel Regardie; second edition, with Wilson's Preface). Closely read: Regardie's Introduction, the opening of the Preface, the eight-circuit overview in Chapter 2, and the opening of Chapter 19. The rest of the book is summarized from its table of contents and general knowledge of the book, so check chapter details against the text. Page numbers below are the printed page numbers from the contents. The book is in copyright and is not hosted here.

WHAT IT IS
A self-help-meets-consciousness-science manual built on the eight-circuit model of the brain. Wilson says in the Preface that it began as a 1978-79 dissertation for Paideia, an alternative university in California, then was rewritten for general readers in Ireland in 1982 with the footnotes removed and exercises added. It is dedicated to Timothy Leary and William S. Burroughs, and the Acknowledgments credit Leary for the eight-circuit model and name Korzybski, Gurdjieff-style self-observation, Crowley and Jung among many influences.

THE CENTRAL IDEA
The brain is hardware running programs, called imprints, which are laid down during sensitive periods and then feel like reality. The book's claim is that most of what we call personality and belief is imprinted programming, and that it can be examined and changed. Each chapter ends with exercises, such as the "why am I here?" meditation in Chapter 2, which asks you to trace the causes of your present moment back through your family, your city and your planet.

THE EIGHT CIRCUITS (as numbered in this book)
1. Bio-survival (oral). Safe or unsafe, nourishing or toxic.
2. Emotional-territorial (anal). Dominance, status, who is top animal.
3. Semantic, or time-binding. Symbols, language, maps, tools.
4. Socio-sexual ("moral"). Roles, mating customs, social rules.
5. Holistic neurosomatic. Body-mind feedback, bliss, "feeling high," tantra. Wilson says it is imprinted by ecstatic experience and that holistic medicine and faith-healing tricks switch it on briefly.
6. Collective neurogenetic. The DNA-brain link; the archetypes of Jung's collective unconscious; numinous and mind-shattering experience.
7. Metaprogramming. The brain reprogramming all other circuits and itself, so that you can choose between "reality tunnels."
8. Non-local quantum. Near-death experience, out-of-body states, precognition, and the quantum non-locality that physicists such as Bell and Bohm describe.
The first four are "terrestrial" circuits, meant for survival on earth. The last four are "extra-terrestrial," a preview of future evolution. Note that Wilson numbers metaprogramming as circuit 7 and the neurogenetic circuit as circuit 6, and that other writers, including Leary in some versions, may swap those two. Check which numbering a source uses.

CHAPTER 19: HOW THE OLD YOGAS MAP ONTO THE CIRCUITS
It opens by reading Patanjali's eight limbs of yoga as a bottom-up reprogramming of the circuits. Posture quiets the survival circuit by monotony. Breath-control mellows the emotional circuit. Concentration or mantra stills the internal monologue of the third circuit. Wilson sets Kabbalah beside Zen koans as ways to exhaust the semantic circuit with unsolvable puzzles. This is typical of the book: it translates old spiritual technologies into its own cybernetic vocabulary.

THEMES
- Reality tunnels. What you perceive is shaped by your imprints.
- Humor as method. Regardie's Introduction praises Wilson's "bubbling humor," and the book's tone is part of its argument.
- Synthesis. Regardie lists the sources: Leary, Gurdjieff, Korzybski, Crowley, yoga, quantum physics.
- A utopian future. Regardie gently says he doubts the speed of Wilson's utopia, and hopes Wilson is right.

HOW IT CONNECTS
- Quantum Psychology is its sequel in spirit.
- Gurdjieff's Beelzebub's Tales and In Search of the Miraculous: the self-observation tradition behind the exercises.
- Dick's Exegesis: both books treat ordinary reality as a program that can be seen through, though Wilson insists on a lighter, more experimental stance than Dick's.
- Regardie wrote the Introduction, so the book sits in the same orbit as the Golden Dawn material on the list.

CAUTIONS
Wilson's science is popularizing, not peer-reviewed, and the book openly mixes models. Treat it as a toolbox and a provocation, not a settled account of the brain.

WHERE TO READ IT
