THE SECRET BOOK OF JOHN (APOCRYPHON OF JOHN)
Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

About these notes: written by Claude in its own words from the text and standard scholarship (Bentley Layton, The Gnostic Scriptures, 1987; James M. Robinson, ed., The Nag Hammadi Library; Karen King, The Secret Revelation of John, 2006; Irenaeus, Against Heresies 1.29, which describes a closely related myth). They are a guide to the text, not a substitute for it. Read the text itself, linked on the Hei page.

WHAT IT IS
A revelation dialogue. After the crucifixion, John, son of Zebedee, is troubled by doubts. The risen Christ appears to him in a shifting form (child, old man, servant) and tells him the truth about the origin of the world and of humanity. It is the fullest surviving statement of what scholars call Sethian Gnostic myth. It survives in four Coptic manuscripts: a short version (Berlin Codex 8502 and Nag Hammadi Codex III) and a long version (Nag Hammadi Codices II and IV). Irenaeus knew a related account around 180 CE, so the Greek original is no later than the second century.

THE STORY IN ORDER
1. The Father. The highest being is an invisible, unnameable Spirit beyond all description. Most of the opening is negative theology: it is not this, not that.
2. Barbelo. The Spirit's thought, reflected in light-water, becomes Barbelo, the first emanation, called Forethought (Pronoia) and the Mother-Father. From the Spirit and Barbelo comes the Only-Begotten, the divine Christ.
3. The fullness. Four great luminaries (Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, Eleleth) and a structured set of aeons fill out the divine realm, called the Pleroma elsewhere in Gnostic literature.
4. Sophia's fall. The last aeon, Sophia ("Wisdom"), wishes to bring forth a being without the consent of the Spirit or her partner. What she produces is malformed: a lion-faced serpent. She hides it in a cloud, away from the other aeons. He is Yaldabaoth, the chief ruler.
5. The false creator. Yaldabaoth steals power from his mother, makes archons and angels, and builds the lower world. In ignorance and pride he declares himself the only God. He says there is no other, echoing the jealous God of Hebrew scripture.
6. The voice from above. A voice answers him, declaring that Man exists and the Son of Man. The archons glimpse a luminous image reflected in the water. To imitate it, they shape the first human being, Adam, from the same likeness.
7. Giving Adam life. Adam lies inert until the powers above send help. By a trick, Yaldabaoth breathes into him and so transfers the borrowed divine power into the body. The archons, furious, throw Adam into matter and into the body made of the four elements.
8. The helper. A spiritual presence called Epinoia of Light is placed in Adam to wake him. She is also Eve in a double sense: the helper in Adam and the woman drawn from him.
9. The serpent and the tree. In this myth the eating of the tree of knowledge is not the sin. The Christ comes in the form of an eagle and urges Adam and Eve to eat. Yaldabaoth is the one who forbids it, because he does not want them to know their origin.
10. Fate and decline. Yaldabaoth and the archons produce Fate (heimarmene) and the chains of the body. Sexual desire, the birth of Cain and Abel, and the later generations follow. Humanity falls further into forgetfulness.
11. Three descents. The Forethought comes down three times to wake the human race. The third descent is described in first person as a voice speaking into the prison of the body, calling the sleeper to remember and be sealed in the water of life.
12. Destiny of souls. Souls that receive the Spirit are saved. Those that know and then turn away face a worse fate than those who never knew. The text ends with John told to write it down and keep it from the unworthy.

KEY IDEAS
- The creator of this world is ignorant and arrogant, not wicked by nature but cut off from the Source.
- The human being contains a spark from the highest realm. Salvation is waking up to that origin, which is knowledge (gnosis).
- Matter and fate are a prison, but they can be left through knowledge, not obedience.
- Scripture is read against the grain: the serpent's offer is the first act of rescue.

HOW IT CONNECTS
- Reality of the Rulers and Pistis Sophia share its cast: Sophia's mistake, the ignorant ruler, the revealer from above.
- Compare Philip K. Dick's VALIS, where a blind, arrogant "demiurge" rules and a "living information" wakes the sleeper. The Exegesis returns to this text's themes constantly.
- Compare the Black Iron Prison and Empire Never Ended with the archons' prison of fate.
