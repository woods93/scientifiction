QUANTUM PSYCHOLOGY
Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

About these notes: written by Claude in its own words. They are based on the table of contents and the opening pages of the owner's copy (Robert Anton Wilson, Quantum Psychology: How Brain Software Programs You and Your World), plus general knowledge of the book. I have not read it chapter by chapter, so check chapter details against the text. The book is in copyright and is not hosted here.

WHAT IT IS
Wilson's attempt to use ideas from twentieth-century physics, general semantics and cybernetics as tools for thinking about the mind. It is a companion to Prometheus Rising, and its chapters, like that book's, end with exercises.

ITS FIVE PARTS (from the contents)
1. How Do We Know What We Know, If We Know Anything? The problem of "deep reality," husband and wife as an analogue of wave and particle, the plurality of selves and universes, the flight from reason, and strange loops and infinite regress.
2. Speaking About the Unspeakable. Quantum logic, the creation of reality-tunnels, and E-Prime, a version of English that avoids the verb "to be." Wilson uses it as a discipline for noticing how language fixes things.
3. The Observer-Created Universe. Psychosomatic synergy and a chapter on taking the mystery out of "miracles."
4. Schroedinger's Cat and Einstein's Mouse. Multiple selves and information systems, multiple universes, and the "Wigner's Friend" puzzle.
5. The Non-Local Self. Hidden variables, quantum futurism, and the journal Trajectories.

THE CORE IDEAS
- Reality-tunnels. Each of us lives inside a model made of language, culture and neural habit. Different tunnels give different universes.
- The observer matters. Wilson borrows the lesson of quantum mechanics that observation is part of the system, and applies it to psychology.
- Model agnosticism. He recommends holding any model loosely, switching between several, and asking what each reveals.
- E-Prime and language discipline. Dropping "is" encourages describing experience and not declaring essences.
- Non-locality. Bell's theorem is used as a metaphor, and sometimes more than a metaphor, for the possibility that mind is not confined to the skull.

HOW IT CONNECTS
- Prometheus Rising supplies the eight circuits. This book mostly develops the seventh and eighth: metaprogramming and the non-local quantum circuit.
- The Exegesis: Dick's "idios kosmos" and "koinos kosmos" (private and common world) are close cousins of Wilson's reality-tunnels.
- Orthogonal Time: Wilson's non-local self and the "whole record" idea both treat time and space as less fixed than the clock suggests.
- Principia Discordia: the same refusal to let any one map be final.

CAUTIONS
Wilson is a popularizer, and the physics is used as inspiration and analogy. Physicists generally do not accept that quantum mechanics proves anything about the mind. Read it for method, not for results.
