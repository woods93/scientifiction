THE REALITY OF THE RULERS (HYPOSTASIS OF THE ARCHONS)
Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

About these notes: written by Claude in its own words from the text and standard scholarship (Bentley Layton, The Gnostic Scriptures, 1987; Roger Bullard, The Hypostasis of the Archons, 1970; James M. Robinson, ed., The Nag Hammadi Library). Read the text itself, linked on the Hei page.

WHAT IT IS
A short text in Nag Hammadi Codex II, probably written in the third century in Greek and surviving in Coptic. It retells Genesis from the Gnostic point of view, briefer and more focused than the Secret Book of John. It assumes you already know the larger myth of Sophia and the false creator, which is why it works best after the Secret Book of John.

HOW IT OPENS
It begins by quoting the letter to the Ephesians: the struggle is not against flesh and blood but against the authorities and powers of the world and the spirits of wickedness. The author says that this text is the explanation of those powers, who they are and where they came from.

THE STORY IN ORDER
1. The ruler. The chief ruler is called Samael ("god of the blind"), also Yaldabaoth and Saklas ("fool"). He is blind by ignorance and arrogance. He declares that he alone is God.
2. The voice. A voice from the incorruptible realm corrects him, saying that he is mistaken. In the Secret Book of John the same moment appears as the reflection that the rulers glimpse.
3. The creation of Adam. The rulers shape Adam from earth in imitation of the image they saw. He lies lifeless until the divine Spirit breathes into him and he becomes a living soul.
4. The spiritual woman. When the rulers see the spiritual presence in Adam, they try to seize it. They put Adam to sleep and open his side, but the spiritual woman escapes them. What they take is a mere imitation, a shadow of her.
5. The pursuit. The rulers pursue the spiritual woman in the form of Eve, but she turns into a tree and leaves her shadow behind. They defile the shadow, and the real Eve is not touched.
6. The serpent. The spiritual woman returns as the serpent, the teacher, and instructs Adam and Eve to eat. In this telling she is a helper, not a tempter.
7. The curse and the expulsion. The rulers curse the woman and the serpent and drive Adam and Eve from paradise into an inferior life.
8. Norea. Eve's daughter Norea resists the rulers when they try to seduce her. She cries out to the God of the entire world for help. The great angel Eleleth answers and tells her her true origin. She is of the root above, not of the rulers.
9. Eleleth's revelation. The angel narrates the history of Sophia, Samael's boast, the rulers' creation, and what will happen at the end: a true human being will be revealed, and the rulers will be destroyed.

KEY IDEAS
- The "authorities" of the world are the rulers and not God. Fighting them is the spiritual task, and the weapon is knowledge.
- Genesis is retold so that the creator is a villain, the serpent a helper, and the woman the carrier of the spiritual principle.
- Norea is the model Gnostic: she recognises her origin and calls on the real God.

HOW IT CONNECTS
- Reads as a companion to the Secret Book of John and to the opening of Pistis Sophia.
- The Ephesians quotation shows how early Christians already spoke of hostile cosmic powers.
- Norea also appears in later traditions (Mandaean and Manichaean).
