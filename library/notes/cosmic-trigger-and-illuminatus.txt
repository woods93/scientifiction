Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

THE ILLUMINATUS! TRILOGY (Robert Shea and Robert Anton Wilson, 1975)
A sprawling comic conspiracy novel in three volumes, in which every secret society, assassination and paranoid theory turns out to be connected, and the connection may itself be a joke. It popularized Discordianism, the goddess Eris, the number 23, and the idea that the world is shaped by competing conspiracies, some of them invented by the people who study them. It was written while both authors worked at Playboy. Read it as satire of conspiracy thinking that also enjoys it, and as the doorway to the Principia Discordia.

COSMIC TRIGGER: THE FINAL SECRET OF THE ILLUMINATI (1977)
Wilson's memoir of a decade of experiments with consciousness: magical practice influenced by Crowley, psychedelics, yoga, and a sequence of strange experiences and coincidences in the 1970s, including what he took to be contact with an intelligence connected to the star Sirius. Wilson presents these experiences as material for investigation, trying several explanations (alien contact, his own unconscious, a dead author's influence, coincidence) without committing to any. That "model agnosticism" is the book's method and its most lasting idea. It also tells of his friendship with Timothy Leary and his involvement in the Illuminati-era counterculture.

WHY BOTH MATTER FOR THE LIST
- They show Wilson's method of treating a mystical experience as a hypothesis, not a creed. That is close to the way Dick's Exegesis tries one explanation after another.
- The Sirius material is a lighter-hearted cousin of Dick's VALIS experience in 1974.
- They lead to Prometheus Rising and Quantum Psychology, which give the same attitude a more systematic form.

CAUTIONS
Wilson is entertaining about conspiracy, and some readers take the jokes as truth. The Illuminati of the books is largely a literary device. Keep that distinction clear when answering anyone who treats the Illuminati as real.
