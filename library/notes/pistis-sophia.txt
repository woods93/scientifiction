PISTIS SOPHIA
Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

About these notes: written by Claude in its own words from the text and standard scholarship. The full text, in G. R. S. Mead's 1921 translation (public domain), is hosted on this site. Further reading: Carl Schmidt and Violet MacDermot, Pistis Sophia (1978); Bentley Layton, The Gnostic Scriptures (1987); Karen King, What Is Gnosticism? (2003).

WHAT IT IS
A long Coptic work of the third or fourth century, surviving in a single manuscript, the Codex Askewianus, now in the British Library. It was bought by Dr. Askew in the eighteenth century and first published in the nineteenth. Mead made the standard English translation in 1896 and revised it in 1921. It belongs to a different branch of Gnostic literature from the Nag Hammadi library, and it is much longer and more ritual in tone.

THE FRAME
The risen Jesus does not leave after the resurrection. He stays with his disciples for eleven years, teaching them. At the end he ascends, then returns, and gives the deeper mysteries. The disciples ask the questions. Mary Magdalene asks more than all the others combined, and Jesus praises her as one whose heart is nearer to the kingdom of heaven than her brothers. Peter complains that she dominates the conversation, and Jesus answers that anyone who is filled with the spirit may speak.

THE CENTRAL STORY
Pistis Sophia ("Faith-Wisdom") is an aeon who longs to reach the highest Light. Deceived by Authades, an arrogant and lion-faced power, she falls into the lower regions and is trapped. The rulers of the lower world torment her and strip her of her light. She repents in thirteen hymns, calling out to the Light. The heavenly Christ comes, rescues her, and sets her on a firm place. The hymns of repentance are interpreted by Jesus' disciples as the Psalms and the Odes of Solomon. The story of Sophia's fall and rescue is the pattern for every soul.

OTHER MAJOR THEMES
- The aeons and the heavens. The cosmos is arranged in layers, each with its rulers. The soul passes up through them after death, and must know the proper names and seals to get by.
- The mysteries. The text describes baptisms, anointings and ritual passwords that free the soul from the power of the rulers and from fate.
- Punishments and rewards. Long passages describe the fate of different souls after death, including the punishments of the wicked and the rebirth of the unrepentant into new bodies.
- The Treasury of the Light. The place where purified light is gathered, and into which the saved are taken.
- Repentance. The repeated emphasis on repentance is unusual for Gnostic texts, which more often stress knowledge.

NOTE ON THE BOOKS OF JEU
The same manuscript tradition includes the Books of Jeu and an untitled text in the Bruce Codex, which present more detailed rituals. They are separate from Pistis Sophia proper.

HOW IT CONNECTS
- The Secret Book of John tells the first fall of Sophia in the heavens. Pistis Sophia tells a second story of her fall and rescue.
- Compare the Hymn of the Pearl: the soul falls into a foreign land, forgets, is called back.
- Mead's Introduction to his edition is a good short guide to the whole Gnostic world, and it is in the hosted text.

WHERE TO READ IT
The whole text is in the Mead Library on this site, as plain text. The Gnostic Society Library has chapter pages with Mead's marginal notes.
