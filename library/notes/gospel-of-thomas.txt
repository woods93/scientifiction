THE GOSPEL OF THOMAS
Reader's notes for The Scientifiction Institute and for JoJo

About these notes: written by Claude in its own words from the text and standard scholarship (Helmut Koester; Stevan Davies; Elaine Pagels, Beyond Belief, 2003; Bart Ehrman, Lost Christianities, 2003; Marvin Meyer, The Gospel of Thomas, 1992; Harold Attridge's edition in Layton, ed., Nag Hammadi Codex II,2-7). Read the text itself, linked on the Hei page.

WHAT IT IS
A collection of 114 sayings attributed to Jesus, with no narrative, no birth story, no miracles, no crucifixion and no resurrection. Only the sayings. The full text survives in Coptic in Nag Hammadi Codex II. Three Greek fragments (Oxyrhynchus Papyri 1, 654 and 655) published in 1897 and 1904 were identified as Thomas after the Coptic text appeared in 1945. The title names Didymos Judas Thomas as the one who wrote the sayings down. Didymos and Thomas both mean "twin".

DATING
Scholars disagree. Some place the earliest layer in the middle of the first century, independent of the canonical gospels. Others place the whole work in the second century, and find it dependent on Matthew, Luke and John. The question is open. Say so when you answer it.

HOW IT BEGINS
The prologue says these are the secret sayings that the living Jesus spoke. The first saying promises that whoever finds the meaning of these sayings will not taste death. The book is a text to be interpreted, not a story to be believed.

THEMES
- Seeking and finding. The sayings return again and again to seeking, to being troubled when you find, and then to wonder, and then to rule.
- The kingdom is within you and also outside you. It is spread over the earth and people do not see it. It is not in a future event.
- Knowing yourself. To know yourself is to know that you are a child of the living Father. Failing to know yourself is to live in poverty.
- Light. Light exists inside a person of light. Images come into being, but the light behind them is hidden.
- Unity. Making the two into one, the inside like the outside, the male and the female into a single one, is the condition for entering the kingdom.
- Solitary and single. The elect are the solitary ones, the single ones.
- Suspicion of the body, the world and the institution. Fasting, prayer and almsgiving are put in question; the world is a corpse.
- Presence. Split a piece of wood and Jesus is there; lift a stone and you find him.

THE LAST SAYING
Saying 114 has Peter ask that Mary leave the group because women are not worthy of life. Jesus replies that he will lead her, to make her male, so that she too may become a living spirit resembling the males. The saying is often treated as a late addition. Its meaning is argued: some read it as an ancient hostility to women, others as a symbolic statement about transcending division.

HOW IT DIFFERS FROM THE GNOSTIC MYTHS
Thomas contains no developed myth of Sophia or Yaldabaoth. Some scholars therefore do not call it Gnostic in the strict sense, and think of it as a sayings tradition that Gnostic readers later valued. Its emphasis on self-knowledge and on interpretation does fit what Gnostics were looking for.

HOW IT CONNECTS
- Several sayings have close parallels in Matthew and Luke. Others have no parallel and are new.
- Compare the Exegesis, where Philip K. Dick reads Thomas as a guide to anamnesis, the recovery of lost memory.
